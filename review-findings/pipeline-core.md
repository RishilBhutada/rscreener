# Review findings: pipeline core (prices, fundamentals, ratios, screener fields)

Read-only review of whole files on `main` (4-Nov-2026), not diffs. Nothing here
has been fixed. Items fixed on 4-Nov-2026 (`git log --since=2026-11-03`) were
skipped.

Files read: `pipeline/fetch_prices.py`, `fetch_bhavcopy.py`, `price_adjust.py`,
`price_periods.py`, `fetch_fundamentals.py`, `fetch_universe.py`, `ratios_lib.py`,
`screen_fields.py`, `seasons_lib.py`, `fund_units.py`,
`fetch_corporate_actions.py`, `fetch_filing_dates.py`, `check_prices.py`,
`check_sources.py`, and the parts of `export_json.py` that use them.

**CONFIRMED** = the code does this; it can be seen by reading the lines named.
**PLAUSIBLE** = the code allows it, but whether it happens depends on what
NSE / BSE / Yahoo actually send, which was not checked against live data.

Ordered by how much it could mislead you, worst first.

| # | file:line | Status | The problem, in plain words | What it means for you | Suggested fix |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `pipeline/fetch_bhavcopy.py:149-193` (with `:165`) | CONFIRMED | The demerger adjustment is worked out once per event and **any failure is stored permanently**. If the exchange file for the ex-date can't be read - a network blip (`:165` turns every error into "no file"), or an ex-date still in the FUTURE (the query at `:149-153` does not limit itself to past dates, and NSE lists announced actions ahead of time) - a row with `applied=0` is written, and the `NOT EXISTS` at `:152` means it is never tried again. | A Vedanta-style demerger announced in advance would never be adjusted: the chart shows a fake crash, every return across the date reads as a loss, and P/E divides the smaller company's price by the old group's earnings. Nothing on the page says so. | Only look at ex-dates before today; on "no exchange file", don't store a row (or store it with a retry-after date) so the next run tries again. |
| 2 | `pipeline/fetch_fundamentals.py:159-200`, `:375-376` | CONFIRMED | "Keep known values" carries forward **every** field Yahoo leaves blank, not just price and market cap. Yahoo blanks `trailingPE` when a company swings to a loss, `dividendYield` when it stops paying, `earningsGrowth` / `revenueGrowth` when they can't be computed - and the old value is kept instead. `fetch_date` is then stamped as new, and `export_json.freshen_prices` (`export_json.py:350-364`) even rescales the old P/E and yield to today's price, so they look current. | A company that has just turned loss-making can keep showing its old positive P/E indefinitely and pass "P/E < 15" screens; a stopped dividend can keep showing a yield. This is the "stale data shown as current" pattern. | Carry forward only the fields that must not vanish (price, market cap, name, sector, website). For ratios, accept Yahoo's blank, or keep the old value only for a few days and mark it. |
| 3 | `pipeline/fetch_fundamentals.py:389-391` (`replace_symbol_rows` `:203-224`) | CONFIRMED | Statements are protected only against a **completely** empty answer. If Yahoo returns some statement types but not others (e.g. the income statement but a throttled balance sheet - `statements_long` silently skips a failing type at `:81-84`), all of the company's stored statement rows are deleted and only the partial set is written back. | That company loses its balance sheet (ROCE, book value, derived ROE, debtor/inventory days, Piotroski) until the next statements refresh, and the run still prints "ok". | Replace rows per (statement type, period type) that actually came back; keep the rest. |
| 4 | `pipeline/fetch_bhavcopy.py:118-133` | CONFIRMED | When adding exchange closes after the stored history, only the FIRST new session's "previous close" is checked against what is stored. Every later session in the window is appended without a check. A split or bonus whose ex-date falls on the 2nd-10th new session is appended **unadjusted** onto Yahoo's split-adjusted history. | A fake 50%/80% one-day crash in the daily series until Yahoo's next full refresh (up to `--max-age-hours`) replaces it: wrong day change, 1M return, 52-week low, RSI/moving averages and volatility for those days. | Check each session's `PrvsClsgPric` against the close written just before it, and stop at the first mismatch (as the index loop at `:287-288` already does). |
| 5 | `pipeline/export_json.py:250-252` | CONFIRMED | The "day change" (`ret_1d`) is the latest close against the **previous stored bar**, whatever its date. A thinly traded company (most BSE-only scrips) may not have traded for weeks, so its "day change" is really a several-week change. The only guard is that the latest bar is within 7 days of the newest close. | Watchlist and home page show a big "today" move on a stock that barely traded, or that moved weeks ago. | Use the exchange's own previous close for the latest session, or require the previous bar to be the previous trading session (or within ~4 days) and otherwise leave it blank. |
| 6 | `pipeline/export_json.py:825-828` | CONFIRMED (missing = zero) / PLAUSIBLE (currency) | EV for the rebuilt EV/EBITDA uses `(debt or 0)` and `(cash or 0)`: a missing debt or cash figure is treated as zero. Separately, the comment above (`:782-790`) says Yahoo's statement figures for New York-listed Infosys/HCLTech can be in US dollars; `totalDebt`/`totalCash` come from the same provider and are not checked for that. | A company whose cash figure is missing gets an EV too high (looks dearer); a missing debt figure makes it look cheaper. If cash is in dollars for Infosys/HCLTech, their EV is overstated by almost the whole cash pile. | Require both figures (else keep the field blank), and apply the same scale check against filed figures that P/S got. |
| 7 | `pipeline/ratios_lib.py:150` | CONFIRMED | PEG = P/E ÷ Yahoo's `earningsGrowth`. As `export_backtest.py` itself notes, that field is the **latest quarter against the same quarter last year**, not a multi-year growth rate. | One unusually good quarter (e.g. +200% from a weak base) produces a tiny PEG that looks like a bargain; PEG screens pick up one-quarter blips. | Divide by a multi-year profit growth (e.g. `profit_cagr_5y` or a 3-year CAGR) and say which on the page. |
| 8 | `pipeline/fetch_filing_dates.py:64-66` vs `:146-149` | CONFIRMED | Two rules contradict each other. In the integrated feed the **revised** date replaces the original broadcast date for a quarter (`:66`); then "earliest announcement wins" (`:148`) - but the original date from that feed is already gone. For quarters only in the integrated feed (FY25 onward), a restated quarter is dated by its restatement. (`fetch_results_history.py:399` does the same.) | The P/E chart and the backtest step those quarters' earnings in later than the market actually saw them - the exact error this file exists to prevent. | Store `broadcast_Date` as the announcement date; keep `revised_Date` separately if wanted. |
| 9 | `pipeline/fetch_prices.py:45-65` | CONFIRMED | Bad-print removal only catches an **upward** spike (`cur/prev > 2.5`); a single bar that collapses and recovers (e.g. 30 between 3,000 and 3,100) is kept. The daily series is not filtered at all. | A downward bad print stays on the Max chart and in the valuation bands (one absurdly low P/E point), the 52-week low and the volatility. | Make the test two-sided, like `trend_lib._spikes`, and apply it to daily bars too. |
| 10 | `pipeline/ratios_lib.py:20-35` | PLAUSIBLE | All ratio inputs are read from the single newest annual date among ALL statement types. If Yahoo has a newer income statement than balance sheet (or the reverse), the items of the lagging statement are simply missing. | ROCE, debtor/inventory days or interest cover blank for those companies, with no hint why. | Take the latest date per statement type, and only combine types whose dates match. |
| 11 | `pipeline/ratios_lib.py:161-163` | CONFIRMED | Two ratios mix periods: dividend payout = latest annual dividends ÷ snapshot net income (trailing 12 months); debtor days = year-end receivables ÷ snapshot revenue (trailing 12 months). | Payout and debtor days are slightly off whenever the latest year and the trailing 12 months differ - most of the year. Small, but not what the label says. | Use the same annual statement for numerator and denominator. |
| 12 | `pipeline/export_json.py:345-364` | PLAUSIBLE | `freshen_prices` rescales market cap and P/E by `new close / snapshot price`. If a split or bonus happened between the snapshot and the newest daily bar, Yahoo's adjusted close is on the new share base while the snapshot's share count and EPS are on the old one. | Until the next snapshot, market cap is shown halved (2:1 split) and P/E halved too. | Skip the rescale (or rescale shares too) when a `splits` row falls between the snapshot date and the bar date. |
| 13 | `pipeline/seasons_lib.py:53`, `:180-186` | PLAUSIBLE | The price-season test drops any quarter holding a corporate action of kind "other". `classify()` in `fetch_corporate_actions.py:84` files every unrecognised subject as "other" (meetings, interest payments, demergers alike). | If many such entries exist (e.g. AGMs, mostly Jul-Sep), those quarters are dropped systematically, biasing the "share-price season" and sometimes hiding it. | Skip only reshaping events: match demerger / amalgamation / capital reduction explicitly. |
| 14 | `pipeline/screen_fields.py:227` | CONFIRMED | Piotroski: a missing long-term-debt line is read as zero debt (stated in the docstring). If debt is reported last year but missing this year, the company scores the "debt went down" point. | F-score can be one point too high on a data gap. | Score that point only when both years report the line (or treat the line as missing only when both years lack it). |
| 15 | `pipeline/fetch_universe.py:96-101` | PLAUSIBLE | A BSE-only company is dropped if its BSE ticker equals any NSE symbol, or if its name matches after dropping words like "India", "Ltd", "Corporation". Two different companies can share either. Also, a partial BSE list is accepted as complete (only an empty one is caught, `:131`). | A real BSE-only company can silently disappear from the universe. | Use the symbol/name match only when the ISIN's company code part also agrees; refuse a BSE list far shorter than the stored one. |
| 16 | `pipeline/fetch_corporate_actions.py:44`, `:81-82` | PLAUSIBLE | Splits: NSE usually words them "Face Value Split (Sub-Division) - From Rs 10/- Per Share To Rs 2/- Per Share", which has no "a:b", so the split's detail is empty. And any subject containing "face value" is classed as a split, including a dividend that mentions face value. | Chart markers show a split with no ratio; a dividend mentioning face value would be shown as a split and its amount lost. | Read "From Rs X To Rs Y" as X:Y; test "dividend" before "face value". |
| 17 | `pipeline/fetch_corporate_actions.py:161` | PLAUSIBLE | Actions are only ever added. If NSE corrects an ex-date or a subject, the old row (different key) stays next to the new one. | Duplicate or wrong-dated dividend/split markers; a wrong-dated demerger row would also be fed to the demerger step (#1). | Per symbol, replace the rows for the date range NSE returned. |
| 18 | `pipeline/seasons_lib.py:189-193` | PLAUSIBLE | The month in progress is stored with its latest session's date, so in Mar/Jun/Sep/Dec a part-month close is taken as that quarter's end. | The current quarter's price-season figure is based on a partial quarter until the month ends. Minor. | Ignore a quarter-end month whose label is not the month's last trading day. |
