# Company page review: `export_company_json.py` and `company/page.tsx`

Read-only review of the company page's figures, on `main` as of 5-Nov-2026 (600f8cbbf).
Scope: `pipeline/export_company_json.py` (whole file) and the parts of
`web/src/app/company/page.tsx` that show what it exports (results tables, TTM column,
ratios, chart data, peer table, shareholding, dividends). Anything fixed on 4/5-Nov-2026 is
left out. Checks against the data used a read-only copy of the production database.

CONFIRMED = seen in the code and, where a quick check was possible, in the data.
PLAUSIBLE = the code allows it; not proven in the data.

| 1 | pipeline/trend_lib.py:984-1014 (used by export_company_json.py:1148-1157); page.tsx:2479-2482 | CONFIRMED | The yearly P&L can switch from consolidated to standalone (or back) part-way down the table. Filed years (up to FY24) are on one basis; FY25 and FY26 are rebuilt from four quarters, which may be on the other basis. The table carries one caption, taken from the newest period, and the page does not show the per-year basis it receives. In the data, 88 companies have their newest filed year on a different basis from their latest four quarters (e.g. CERA, JYOTHYLAB, SANOFI, MANYAVAR: consolidated years, standalone quarters; TIMKEN, AGI, KIRLPNU: the reverse). | For these companies the yearly table, its growth and its CAGRs compare two different businesses (with and without subsidiaries) as if they were one, under a caption that is right for only part of the table. | When the rebuilt years' basis differs from the filed years', either rebuild from quarters on the filed basis (if stored) or mark the switch in the table (a basis row or a divider) and stop growth/CAGR across it. |
| 2 | export_company_json.py:647-661 | CONFIRMED (code) | "CFO / EBITDA %" divides Yahoo's cash from operations by the filed **Operating Profit** (other income left out), not by the EBITDA row the P&L shows (operating profit + other income). | 3 | export_company_json.py:306-312 | CONFIRMED | Piotroski test 7, "Owners not diluted", compares Yahoo's share count year on year. A bonus issue or a split raises the count, so the test fails ("+100.0% shares outstanding") although no owner was diluted. In the data: AGIIL, BTML and DAVANGERE each have a split or bonus on record and Yahoo's count jumps 40-410% in the latest year. | Companies that gave a bonus or split last year lose a point and are told they diluted shareholders, which is false. | Divide each year's count by the split/bonus factor (`adj_factor`) before comparing, as the EPS rows already do. |
